# Executive Deck Verbiage Rules

This guide is mandatory for executive/stakeholder decks.

## Primary Rule: Describe, Do Not Judge

When describing current state, avoid language that sounds dismissive of the people in the room.

Do not use:
- manual
- slow
- ad hoc
- broken
- primitive
- messy

Prefer:
- "draws on deep regional knowledge"
- "coordinates across many stakeholders"
- "supports nuanced exceptions"
- "operates through established local workflows"

Then describe the opportunity with neutral precision.

## Lead With The So What

Each slide should answer:
- Why this matters now
- What decision or implication follows

If a sentence does not help a decision-maker act or align, cut it.

## Keep Language Concrete

Prefer concrete nouns and observable facts over abstractions.

Less effective:
- "optimize cross-functional enablement"

More effective:
- "reduce handoffs across finance, legal, and operations from six to three"

## Keep Parallel Slides Grammatically Parallel

For sets of similar slides:
- Use matching section headers
- Use matching bullet grammar
- Use matching component labels

Parallel language makes differences legible without editorializing.

## Do Not Pre-Bake Conclusions

If a workshop or working session is intended to produce decisions, do not present conclusions as already settled.

Use language like:
- "questions to resolve"
- "signals observed"
- "options to evaluate"

## Be Honest About Readiness

Use explicit readiness labels:
- Ready now
- Partial / in progress
- Needs building

Do not overstate maturity to make a slide look complete.

## Tone

- Respectful
- Precise
- Decision-oriented
- Calm and confident

Avoid hype, dramatic claims, or blame framing.

## Editing Checklist Before Finalizing Copy

- Current-state language respects operators and stakeholders
- Every slide has a clear "so what"
- Filler words and vague abstractions removed
- Parallel slides use parallel grammar
- Readiness statements are explicit and honest
